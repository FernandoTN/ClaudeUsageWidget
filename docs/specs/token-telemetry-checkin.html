Token usage window — design check-in

Token-telemetry sibling · 2026-09-04 · five decisions, each with a marked recommendation. Reply in the session with your picks (or just “go with the recommendations”). Everything measured below was read from this Mac tonight, read-only; consult reviews from Codex gpt-5.6-sol and Grok are running and their findings will be folded into the spec before stage 1 starts.

What this window is — and is not

It shows consumption: how many tokens the fleet actually sent and received, read from the CLIs’ own local logs, by provider, model, account and day. It is not quota — the providers weigh those tokens their own way, and the bars in the menu bar keep answering “how much is left”. Every headline in the window carries its source and its age. Nothing leaves the machine.

Full write-ups: docs/research/2026-09-04-token-telemetry-sources.md (the data) · docs/specs/token-telemetry.md (the proposal).

1. Layout

Three ways to lay out one window. All three share the same report content (KPI tiles, the daily chart, by-model and by-account tables, a provenance footer); they differ in how you get to a provider or an account.

A — Report page

Filters in the header (provider chips, an account pop-up). Simplest build; everything in one glance.

Weak spot: a pop-up of 24 account names, and nowhere for an account’s own switch timeline.

RECOMMENDEDB — Source list + report

The sidebar is the filter: Fleet → each provider → its accounts → “Unattributed”. The chart’s stacking follows the level (Fleet: per provider; a provider: by model; an account: by model), so the drill-down reads the same at every depth. The inspector’s “Usage history” link simply selects a row.

Costs a wider minimum window (880 pt). Scales to the 30–40 accounts the UX spec plans for.

NOT RECOMMENDEDC — Tabs

Overview / Models / Accounts / Timeline. Every comparison the owner asked for (“per provider or the aggregate… trends… through time”) needs two tabs open at once.

B, frame by frame — Fleet scope, last 7 days, real numbers from tonight’s census

  1. 1Header. Scope name from the sidebar; window control Today · 7 days · 30 days · All indexed; one provenance line — “consumption… not quota… indexed 12 s ago” — the only place the disclaimer lives. Never a banner.
  2. 2KPI tiles. Input-class with its cache-read share (the single most important disclosure: 97 % of the load is cache traffic), output with its thinking share, units with the subagent share, and a labelled list-price estimate. Hover: one tooltip with every series and the total. Markers under the axis: ⇄ switches, × rate-limit stops (stage 4).
  4. 4Two tables. By model (share bar, cached %, ≈ cost) and by account (name, provider mark on the current owner, “Active for Claude” badge, share, last active). The last row is Unattributed (before …) — shown, never redistributed. In an account scope the second table becomes Switches.

2. Default time window

RECOMMENDED7 days

Long enough for a trend and the weekly rhythm; short enough that a switch two days ago is still on screen. The KPI deltas read “vs prior 7 days”.

30 days

Matches the CLI’s retention window; day columns get thin (30 × 24 pt in 670 pt fits, but tight). Deltas “vs prior 30 days” need 60 days of index — empty for the first two months.

Today

Hourly columns. Useful for “what is happening right now”, but that question the bar already answers; opening on today hides the trend.

3. The cost figure

RECOMMENDEDShown, labelled “≈ at list price”

Subscriptions are flat, so this is an API-equivalent figure, not a bill — but it is the only common unit across Opus/Fable/Sonnet and across providers, and it is what makes “impact” legible: this week’s 78 B cache reads are ≈ $58 k of API. The ⓘ opens the shipped price table with its as-of date; Grok uses the CLI’s own figure and says so.

Behind a toggle

Tokens by default; cost when asked. Keeps the first screen purely factual. Costs a setting and a fifth tile state.

Omit

No estimate at all. Avoids any reading of “we spent $58 k”; loses the cross-model comparison.

4. What the first index imports

RECOMMENDEDEverything on disk (≈ 27 GB)

Claude’s 30 days (the CLI deletes older transcripts, so this is the only chance to keep them), Codex since May 14 including the 11 GB July archive (50 B tokens on two days), Grok’s 30 days. ~65 s of CPU spread over bounded ticks. “All indexed” then means something on day one. The July spike is why the chart auto-splits when one column exceeds 20× the median.

Last 30 days only

Smaller first pass (≈ 16 GB), no July outliers. Loses Codex history that exists nowhere else once the index starts governing what is kept.

5. Markers on the timeline (stage 4)

RECOMMENDEDSwitches and rate-limit stops

⇄ for every switch of the row’s provider (who → who, manual/auto/queued, the reason), × for every Claude transcript death on the limit (2,228 on disk). Together they explain the dips: a burn that stops at × and resumes on another account after ⇄ is the fleet doing its job — or not.

Switches only

Cleaner axis. The rate-limit stops already feed the bar’s tripwire; showing them here is the first time they become visible as history.
