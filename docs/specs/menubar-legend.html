What everything on the bar means

The legend for the menu bar, the ⇄ menu, the popover and the dashboard. One colour always means one thing, on every surface. Keep this open; it is self-contained.

1 · Colours — the same everywhere, in your words

One rule across all three hues: bright means more relief is available — more of the week left, or the reset coming sooner. Faded means less. "Hit" always means the server-affirmed value at or over the auto-switch threshold; an inferred stamp never counts (that is purple).

ColourMeans, exactly

light redThe weekly or Fable limit is hit, and the reset is more than 24 hours away.
× red crossSomething is wrong with the account itself: a dead login, a revoked token, the CLI cannot use it. Repair in Accounts.

faded orangeThe 5-hour session limit is hit, and weekly or Fable has 50 % or less left.

light greenSession available, weekly or Fable has 50 % or less left.

hollow greyNever measured.
– grey dashExcluded from the auto-switch: the per-account toggle is off, or it is a free plan.

The cut-offs — 24 hours and 50 % — are two constants in one place (ReadinessThresholds.weeklyResetSoon, comfortableRemaining), and the same roles drive the bar's dots, the counts row, the dashboard's chips and roster, the Accounts inspector and the ⇄ menu.

2 · The fleet bar (your layout: "Active + dots")

One tile per provider. Left: the active account's own tile (its bars and label, as before). Right: the fleet block for the other accounts of that provider.

Anatomy of the fleet block

  1. Cl / Cx / Gk — the provider (Claude, Codex, Grok). Under it, the provider's total number of accounts. "Cl 19" with 18 dots means: 18 others here, the 19th is the tile on the left.
  2. One dot per other account, ordered by its weekly reset — every provider, every state: right = resets soonest, left = farthest from its weekly reset; accounts with no known reset (never measured, or a dead login with nothing cached) sit leftmost, together. The top row runs right→left from the soonest, then the bottom row continues; the order is stable between sweeps (ties break by name), so dots never jump. Two rows from four accounts; twenty dots at most, then +N for the rest (the farthest resets, the least decision-relevant ones).
  3. Bottom row — the hand-off, shown only when it matters (the active account is at 75 % of its window, or you queued a target): 78 the active account's usage → dJo the next account (three letters) ✓ its login was verified live. It stays blank while nothing is armed. A block is exactly as wide as its dots, never padded for this row: a wide block (Claude) shows the whole row, a narrow one shows just "→dJo", and a very small one (Codex with four dots) leaves the hand-off to the ⇄ menu, where it always is.

The order, on a real block

Left: farthest from its weekly reset · … · right: resets soonest. Top row first, then the bottom row, both right to left. The soonest-to-reset account is the one the auto-switch ranks first, which is why it sits at the edge you look at. The hand-off name underneath ("dJo") takes the candidate's readiness colour — green there means that account is ready, not the active one.

What a dot can look like

Exactly the colours of section 1 — every dot is one of the ten states above. A dimmed dot means its reading is older than ten minutes; the colour is kept until a new measurement arrives.

A dot changes colour only on real evidence: a new measurement, a login dying or reviving, a server-affirmed limit, a window resetting, an account being excluded or included. Time passing never repaints a dot, and a suspicion has to persist for a minute before a dot turns purple.

The bottom row, all forms

78 →dJo ✓ — armed; next is dJo…, login verified.

78 →jsk — a red arrow: your queued target cannot be used right now, this is the ranked fallback. A blue arrow would mean "this is your queued target". No ✓ means the login has not been verified yet.

→— — armed, and nobody has headroom. The dashboard's status strip says the same.

+6 — six more accounts than the twenty dots can show.

The other fleet layout, "Active + counts"

Instead of dots, one row of counts: ●3 session available (both greens) · ◐N session hit (both oranges) · ▲7 weekly or Fable hit (both reds) · ×1 dead. Settings → Profiles → Multi-Profile Display → Menu bar layout switches between the layouts; "Every account" is the old bar.

Hover any provider tile: the tooltip spells all of this out in words, and ends with the legend.

3 · The active tile (the left part, and every tile in "Every account")

  • Bars — top: the 5-hour session; below: the weekly window (and Fable's weekly window where the style shows it). Length = usage.
  • Tick on a bar — how far the window's time has elapsed. Bar ahead of the tick = burning faster than time passes.
  • Bar colour — pace, projected to the end of the window: comfortable (under 50 % projected) · on track (50–75 %) · warming (75–90 %) · pressing (90–100 %) · critical (100–120 %) · runaway (over 120 %). Green, then amber, then red.
  • Label — the account name. Cyan: this account is active for its provider. Light red: a weekly window is at its limit ("unusable this week", the more urgent fact, wins over cyan). Purple: suspected throttle, the number is the last measured one.
  • Dimmed tile — the active account's reading is older than ten minutes.

5 · The click: the fleet dashboard

  • Header — accounts, when the numbers were updated, the last switch. Icons: refresh · token usage · settings.
  • Status strip — one line per condition, ordered: blocking (red, e.g. preferences unavailable) · needs action (amber, e.g. "3 dead logins — repair in Accounts") · information (grey, e.g. "Codex: nowhere to switch to", "not visible in the menu bar").
  • Section header — CLAUDE · 10 accounts, "Viewing X · Active for Claude: Y" (Viewing = the account you are looking at; Active = the one that owns the login; "· pinned" = you chose it, the auto-switch will not move it), the counts in words, and ⇄ to open the menu on this provider.
  • ACTIVE card — the owner's gauges with "resets in…", the age of the reading (or "via API headers" / "CLI cache" when it was not measured with the account's own credentials), the suspected caveat, and the ETA to the auto-switch threshold at the current pace.
  • Next / Queue — the predicted target with its evidence; the queue; the thresholds the auto-switch fires at.
  • ROSTER — the other accounts, soonest weekly reset first, three rows then "Show all N". Each row: the dot glyph and name; tags next, queued #1, re-login needed, same account as … (two profiles, one quota); S/W/F percentages; on the right, the state and the reading's age. Click a row for the account's details; right-click for Make active, Queue next, Repair, Token usage.
  • INSIGHTS (collapsed) — resets in the next 7 days, blind spots, changes made outside the app, the switch log, burn rate, rate-limit incidents, capacity remaining, why the others cannot be switched to.

7 · Switching by hand

In the classic popover, the name menu only views an account. When the viewed account is not the owner, "Make active for Claude… · now X" appears.

The confirmation names both sides with their headroom and states the cost. A dead login cannot be switched to ("Log in first"). After a switch the row reads "Active for Claude: X ✓ · just now".

Sentences you will see: "Switched to X" · "X is already active" · "X's login is dead — the CLI login was not changed. Log in again, then Sync" · "X is focused, but its login is dead so the CLI login was not changed".

Settings → Profiles → Multi-Profile Display: Menu bar layout (Every account · Active + dots · Active + counts) and Click opens (Follow layout · Fleet dashboard · Account popover). Auto-switch thresholds: Settings → Active & Auto-switch.
