What the strip must answer (first principles)

  1. Is the account my CLI sessions are on about to stall? → the provider-active account's session window (5 h). Weekly/Fable drive the 99 % switch.
  2. When it stalls, is there somewhere to go, and where? → the predicted next candidate and whether its login was proven live (a probe, a refresh, a real switch), or a queued hand-off.
  3. Is anything broken only I can fix? → dead logins, a blind/suspected active account (purple), cfprefsd degraded, stale data.
  4. How much of the fleet is still usable this week? → ready vs exhausted — a pattern or a count, not 22 percentages.

Per-account percentages, reset times, per-model windows and pace ticks move one click away. One summary tile per provider (Claude, Codex, Grok), in the same three status items the app already has, because each provider has exactly one active login and the auto-switch never crosses providers.

Decision 1 — Menu-bar layout

RECOMMENDED B · Active tile + fleet dots ≈ 226 pt total, fixed

Per provider: the active account's existing tile (unchanged renderer, same bars, pace ticks, purple/red tints; dimmed when its reading is stale) + a fleet block: a small provider mark (Cl / Cx / Gk), one 4 pt mark per other account of the provider filled column-major from the right edge so the rightmost column is always the soonest weekly reset ("next to burn") even when 17 accounts wrap to two rows, and the candidate row (decision 2) on the label row. Past 20 accounts the two leftmost columns become "+N". The block's width is fixed for a given roster (the wider of the dots and the reserved candidate row), so arming never moves your other menu items. Grok with one account is just its tile. You still see the fleet: "mostly red, four green, one ring, two ×" reads without a digit.

ready · low (session ≥ 80 % or weekly/Fable ≥ 90 %; still a valid switch target) · exhausted (session ≥ 95 %, weekly/Fable ≥ 99 %, or server-affirmed throttle) · suspected (inferred throttle; the dashboard shows the last measured value) · × dead login (needs /login) · – excluded from auto-switch (toggle off / free plan) · never measured · any mark at 50 % = reading older than 3 min

A · Active tile + fleet counts ≈ 266 pt total, fixed

Same active tile and candidate row; the matrix becomes one row of counts: ● ready, ◐ low, ▲ exhausted, ✕ dead — never merged (low is still a valid target, exhausted is not). It loses the ordering and the pattern, and because the row must reserve room for two-digit counts it is not narrower than the dots for this roster. Selectable in Settings as a style preference.

C · One combined text item — dropped after the consult

Everything in one status item as coloured digits. All three reviewers rejected it: not narrower than "active blocks only", loses the bars and pace ticks (the one thing that shows the burning account climbing), overflow becomes all-or-nothing, and folding three status items into one changes the item set (the CAContext leak the composite mode exists to avoid). Not mocked up.

Decision 2 — Show the NEXT candidate in the bar?

The fleet block's bottom row shows digits → name ✓ while armed: the active account passed 75 % of its keyed window (the preflight's own milestone), a hand-off is queued, or — at any usage — there is nobody to go to. Digits are the active account's last measured / projected value (never a stamp's 100; purple when suspected). The name is tinted by the candidate's quota evidence (green ready, orange low, grey stale); the glyph by its login evidence: ✓ only when a probe, a refresh or a real switch proved the login (every sweep's 200 counts), ? when only an expiry check or an inconclusive probe exists, × when dead. A red Q means your queued hand-off is blocked and the ranked fallback is shown. The row is 7 pt and its widest form ("99Q→WWW✓") was measured against the real font; nothing changes the tile's width.

Decision 3 — The dashboard (one click away)

RECOMMENDED D1 · Fleet-board popover, 380 pt, scrollable, detachable

Stacked provider sections with sticky headers (not tabs — a fleet board shows all three groups; it opens scrolled to the provider you clicked). Each section: an Active card naming the account that owns that provider's CLI login, session / weekly / Fable bars with reset countdowns and which window fires the switch, the suspected caveat with the last measured value and its age when relevant, Next with what the verdict proved and how old both the login and the quota evidence are, the provider's slice of the queue (read-only, link to Settings), and the ETA to the threshold from the burn projection. A roster in the bar's order with two-line rows: identity, state chip and chevron on line one; session / weekly / Fable and the reset on line two — one line does not fit at 380 pt. Tap a row for the account detail (today's per-account view, extracted into a reusable component). Row menu: Make active… (a two-step inline confirmation quoting the switch cost, through the same gate the auto-switch uses, so a dead login reads as "login dead" and never as a silent no-op), Queue next, Open in Settings. Recent switches collapsed at the bottom. Dragging the popover off the bar turns it into a floating panel (already supported; the panel and popover get their own sizes).

D2 · Dedicated dashboard window (sidebar + table + detail pane)

More room and it persists, but it needs the Settings window's activation-policy care, a second window lifecycle to keep orphan-free, and it does not close on an outside click — the wrong default for a glance surface. Deferred until the detached panel is genuinely too small (30+ accounts).

D1 (recommended)D2Both (D1 now, D2 later)

Decision 4 — What a click on a provider tile does

Recommended: any click on a provider's summary tile opens the dashboard on that provider (stage A, before the dashboard exists: today's popover for that provider's active account, with the navigator still walking every member). The dots are 4 pt and not a sane click target; the dashboard roster is the lookup. The "Every account" layout keeps today's per-tile click routing for anyone who wants it.
